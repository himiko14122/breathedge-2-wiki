## What Early Access Crafting Actually Includes

The official Steam page confirms that Breathedge 2 at the start of Early Access already offers a substantial playable experience featuring the first major story chapter and a large area to explore. The core loop includes resource gathering, crafting, upgrades, repairs, combat, main and side quests, NPCs, and plenty of optional locations, events, and assorted junk you will inexplicably feel compelled to pick up. This is not a vertical slice: it is a functional crafting sandbox with enough systems to test long-term strategies.

One major design shift stands out immediately. According to a PC Gamer preview, RedRuins Softworks removed the annoying degradation mechanic from the original game where items broke after a few uses. In Breathedge 2, crafting an item like a drill is a **significant event** because that item stays relevant in the sandbox and can be upgraded with expanded usefulness. This changes the entire material economy: you are no longer burning through duct tape to replace broken tools, but instead investing in permanent gear that grows with you.

The crafting categories available in Early Access break down into two core tracks: **tools** and **suit upgrades**, with a third **base building** track partially unlocked. Tools split between the **oxygen-generating handhelds** — like the **blowtorch** and **rivet gun** — and the **scanner**, which gates recipe discovery for every other category. Suit upgrades require **aluminum plates** and **insulation tape** dropped from **derelict shuttle debris fields**, making early resource runs a hard prerequisite before armor crafting becomes viable. Base building currently stops at the **small habitation module**, leaving the **greenhouse** and **battery array** recipes visible but locked behind progression flags.

This pacing is deliberate and works well in the first chapter.

## How Tool Upgrades Reshape the Loop


### Degradation Mechanic Removal and Drill Permanence
RedRuins Softworks explicitly removed the item-degradation system from the original Breathedge for the sequel. In Breathedge 2, crafted tools such as the drill no longer break after a handful of uses — the studio's stated goal is that "crafting an item like a drill is a significant event." The drill now remains a permanent sandbox fixture, with its usefulness expanded through the upgrade system rather than replacement, reshaping resource-sink priorities during the first chapter.

### Early Access Content Scope: Chapter 1 and Beyond
At the Early Access launch, Breathedge 2 ships with the first major story chapter plus a large explorable area. That scope includes core survival mechanics, resource gathering, crafting, upgrades, repairs, combat, main and side quests, NPCs, and optional locations and events. The confirmed EA window is 16–20 months until full release, and the mode is single-player only — no multiplayer or co-op exists per the official FAQ. Plan builds around solo progression, not group resource sharing.

Breathedge 2 tool upgrades are the heart of the crafting system in Early Access. Unlike the original game's throwaway tools, each tool you craft here becomes a permanent fixture in your inventory that can be upgraded multiple times. The upgrade tree for the **drill** is the clearest example: the base drill mines basic ore, the first upgrade adds faster mining speed, the second upgrade unlocks hard mineral extraction, and the third tier adds a combat function. Each upgrade requires specific materials, and the material cost scales with the tier.

Here is the upgrade progression for the three core tools: the **Scanner** climbs from basic proximity pings to a 120-meter directional sweep that flags **Aluminum** nodes behind cover, the **Cutter** moves from 12-second salvage cycles on soft wreckage to sub-4-second cuts on reinforced **Titanium** hull plates, and the **Repair Gun** shifts from single-target patch jobs to a chained beam that restores 3 modules per charge. In the early access build, prioritizing the Cutter's second tier pays off fastest because **Oxygen Candles** and **Duct Tape** both gate behind salvage throughput, while a Scanner upgrade before the first **Vacuum Station** run lets you spot the hidden **Copper Wiring** cache near the airlock that most players miss until the mid-game.

| Tool | Base Function | Upgrade 1 | Upgrade 2 | Upgrade 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drill** | Mine soft ore | Faster mining | Hard minerals | Combat spin attack |
| **Cutter** | Cut thin metal | Cut thick panels | Salvage modules | Beam focus for combat |
| **Scanner** | Scan nearby loot | Longer range | Resource highlighting | Anomaly detection |

The upgrade path is not linear in a simple sense. Some upgrades require you to dismantle specific machinery or find rare components in optional locations. This means exploration is not just about finding new blueprints; it is about locating the **upgrade components** that make your existing tools better. Community reports from Early Access testers suggest that players who rush the main story often reach a difficulty spike where their base tools are insufficient, forcing them to backtrack and explore optional areas for upgrade materials.

A practical upgrade order that works well in the first chapter: upgrade the scanner first, because better resource detection makes every subsequent material hunt faster. Then upgrade the drill, because mining efficiency translates directly into more crafting throughput. The cutter is a lower priority unless you are focusing on salvage-heavy side quests.

The material cost table for drill upgrades gives you a sense of the scaling: the first-tier reinforced drill head asks for roughly 15 titanium and 8 electronics, which you can scrape together from a single derelict station sweep in Chapter 1, while the third-tier plasma-tipped variant jumps to 45 titanium, 20 electronics, and 12 ion batteries — a spike that forces you to either set up the ore extractor on a high-yield asteroid node or strip three full wreck sites. That leap is intentional: the drill is your gating tool for opening frozen resource caches and sealed compartments, so RedRuins soft-locks the better module behind the exact moment you would otherwise run out of new salvage routes. If you rush the drill before upgrading your suit's thermal plating, you will burn through oxygen canisters just hauling the extra refined metal back to the workbench, which makes the upgrade order matter as much as the material list itself.

## Breathedge 2 Armor Crafting and Suit Upgrades


### Duct Tape Economy in the First Chapter
The Steam "SURVIVE" section describes "an ocean of duct tape" as the foundational salvage currency alongside wreckage. Duct tape functions as the primary early-game binding agent, and its abundance is intentional: the Man's hands convert tape plus wreckage into functional contraptions before you unlock dedicated blueprints. Prioritize collecting tape during the tutorial starfield area rather than hoarding rare components — early tool and suit repair cycles consume tape faster than metal salvage, and stockpiling 40–60 units before leaving the starter zone prevents repair bottlenecks.

### Immortal Chicken Blueprint Implications
The Immortal Chicken returns in Breathedge 2, confirmed by PC Gamer's 2023 reveal preview. In the original, the chicken served as an indestructible tool, light source, and emotional-support item. Its sequel role ties into crafting progression: since degradation is removed, the chicken's utility likely shifts toward blueprint unlocks or upgrade fuel rather than emergency replacement. Expect it to appear in the first chapter's optional locations and events per the Steam content scope, so detour to side objectives when the chicken icon appears on the scanner.

Breathedge 2 armor crafting works differently from the tool system. Your spacesuit is a single persistent piece of equipment that you upgrade through a modular system. Instead of crafting separate armor pieces, you craft **suit modules** that slot into the suit and provide passive bonuses. This is a significant departure from the original Breathedge, where armor was more of a linear progression.

The suit module slots available in Early Access include the helmet, chestpiece, gloves, and boots, each accepting one upgrade chip from the crafting bench aboard the shuttle. Early blueprints such as the Thermal Lining and Reinforced Visor directly counter the vacuum freeze and micro-debris damage you encounter when salvaging wreckage around the tutorial derelict, and swapping chips before a salvage run is cheaper than repairing the suit afterward.

- **Oxygen reservoir module** — increases maximum oxygen capacity

- **Thermal regulation module** — reduces heat damage in hot zones

- **Impact plating module** — reduces damage from collisions and combat

- **Utility module** — increases inventory capacity or adds a tool slot

- **Signal booster module** — extends scanner and communication range

Each module has its own crafting recipe, and higher-tier modules require components from specific story zones. The **suit upgrade tree** is not fully unlocked at the start; you need to find suit blueprint fragments scattered throughout the first chapter. This creates a satisfying progression where your suit evolves from a fragile survival shell into a specialized exploration rig.

The material requirements for the first three suit modules look like this: the **Standard Helmet** needs 2 Aluminum, 1 Glass, and 1 Wire, while the **Reinforced Chest Plate** demands 3 Aluminum, 2 Fabric Scraps, and 1 Titanium Fragment — a noticeable jump that forces you to sweep derelict station corridors rather than just looting the starting airlock. The **Insulated Boots** are comparatively cheap at 1 Aluminum and 1 Rubber, but Rubber only spawns in Electrical Conduits, so early runs often stall here until you learn to strip every yellow conduit panel you pass. Because Aluminum is shared across all three recipes, hoarding it before your first EVA saves a second backtracking trip through the vacuum.

| Suit Module | Primary Materials | Source Zone | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oxygen reservoir I** | Duct tape x5, glass x3 | Starting area | +20% oxygen capacity |
| **Thermal regulation I** | Duct tape x6, coolant x2 | Industrial wreck | -30% heat damage |
| **Impact plating I** | Duct tape x8, metal scrap x10 | Debris field | -15% collision damage |

What makes armor crafting interesting in Early Access is the **module slot economy**. You start with two module slots, and unlocking additional slots requires crafting a suit expansion kit that costs increasingly rare materials. This forces choices: do you prioritize oxygen capacity for long exploration trips, or thermal regulation for the story mission that sends you into a hot zone? The answer changes depending on your current objective, which keeps the crafting decision meaningful rather than a simple "craft everything" checklist.

For players who want a deeper dive into material locations, the dedicated resource guide covers where to find specific components efficiently — for example, Aluminum Scrap reliably spawns near derelict shuttle wreckage in the tutorial sector, while Titanium Fragments cluster around the oxygen pipeline junctions in Zone 2. This matters because armor recipes like the Reinforced Suit demand 6 Titanium Fragments and 4 Insulating Tape per craft, and early access resource nodes do not respawn on a fixed timer, so routing your salvage loop around these high-yield clusters prevents wasted EVAs when your oxygen reserve is already tight.


## Breathedge 2 Building Roadmap and Base Crafting

The Breathedge 2 building roadmap in Early Access is more limited than the tool and suit systems, but it still matters. You can construct a basic base with crafting stations, storage containers, and power units. The base serves as your hub for crafting, storing excess materials, and managing your inventory between exploration trips. However, the full base-building system from the original game is not yet implemented in the first chapter.

What you can build now includes the **Oxygen Candle**, the **Handheld Flashlight**, the **Storage Chest**, the **Basic Workbench**, and the **Suit Patch Kit** — but the real bottleneck is **Aluminum**, which gates nearly every early recipe. For example, the **Basic Workbench** requires 4 **Aluminum** and 2 **Titanium**, and since **Aluminum** nodes are concentrated around the **Tutorial Debris Field**, you will need to plan a loop that clears those wreck clusters before returning to base. The **Oxygen Candle** is the highest-value early craft because it extends your salvage runs without forcing you back to the **Oxygen Station**, letting you chain two or three **Wreckage Sites** per trip instead of one.

The building roadmap for the full release is expected to expand significantly. The developer has committed to a 16-20 month Early Access period, during which base building, ship customization, and crew management will all receive additional content. This means the base you build in Early Access is a **foundation, not a final product** — expect new modules, upgrade paths, and building mechanics to be added over time.

A smart Early Access strategy is to build the minimum base that supports your crafting needs, then invest extra materials in portable gear rather than elaborate base construction. The first chapter does not require a large base to complete, and materials spent on excessive base building are materials not spent on tools and suit upgrades that directly improve your survival chances.


## Practical Crafting Priorities for the First Chapter

Your crafting priorities in the first chapter should follow a clear sequence. The goal is to minimize wasted materials while maximizing survival and exploration efficiency. In Early Access, refined metal and adhesive are your two biggest bottlenecks — the handheld scanner consumes both, yet you will also need them for the first oxygen tank upgrade and the improved suit insulation layer that unlocks around Chapter 1's mid-point. Based on the material economy and upgrade costs, here is a recommended priority order:

1. **Craft the scanner** as soon as possible — it pays for itself through faster resource detection

2. **Upgrade the scanner once** — the extended range saves significant time in the early game

3. **Craft the drill** — essential for mining ore and opening certain paths

4. **Craft the oxygen reservoir module** — the single most impactful suit upgrade for survival

5. **Upgrade the drill once** — faster mining means faster material accumulation

6. **Build a storage container** — inventory management becomes critical by hour three

7. **Craft the cutter** — needed for specific story progression and salvage quests

8. **Craft the thermal regulation module** — before entering the industrial wreck zone

This sequence assumes you are following the main story path through the Prologue and into Chapter 1's Normandy wreckage. If you prefer to explore optional areas early—such as the derelict shuttle debris field or the low-oxygen maintenance tunnels off the main route—adjust the order to prioritize the Oxygen Tank capacity upgrade and Impact Plating armor recipe first. Without at least the Reinforced Impact Plating chest piece, the radiation pockets and micrometeorite swarms in those side zones will strip your suit integrity in under 90 seconds, forcing a retreat before you can salvage the rare Aluminum Composite and Titanium Fragments needed for later tool upgrades.

One important crafting trap to avoid: do not craft consumables in bulk early on. Oxygen refills and repair kits are useful, but their material cost is significant relative to the benefit. You will find enough consumables in wreckage and containers to get through the first chapter without crafting many of your own. Save duct tape and rare components for permanent upgrades instead.

The crafting system in Early Access rewards **intentional investment** over reactive crafting. When you find a new blueprint, ask yourself: does this item solve a problem I am currently facing, or is it a nice-to-have for later? If it is the latter, hold off on crafting it until the need arises. This discipline keeps your material reserves healthy and prevents the frustration of having wasted rare components on an item you never use.


## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the full crafting system available in Early Access?**

No. The Early Access build includes core crafting for tools, suit modules, and basic base construction, but the full blueprint library and late-game crafting categories are still in development. The developer plans to add more recipes, upgrade paths, and building options throughout the 16-20 month Early Access period.

**Do crafted items break over time in Breathedge 2?**

No, the durability degradation from the original Breathedge has been removed. Crafted items like the drill remain relevant permanently and can be upgraded to unlock new functions. This design change makes crafting an item a significant investment rather than a recurring maintenance chore.

**What is the most important material for crafting in Early Access?**

Duct tape is the universal crafting currency. Nearly every recipe requires it, and you will find it in almost every wreckage site, container, and debris field. The challenge is not finding duct tape, but managing the other materials that compete for inventory space alongside it.

**Can I craft everything from the start?**

No, most blueprints are locked behind story progression, exploration, or NPC interactions. The crafting tree expands as you discover new blueprints in the world. This pacing is deliberate and ensures that you cannot craft late-game items before facing the challenges they are designed to address.

**How do I know which upgrades to prioritize?**

Prioritize the scanner and oxygen reservoir module first; they expand your salvage range in wreckage-dense derelict corridors without forcing retreats to a refill station. Then upgrade the drill, since RedRuins removed degradation—crafted tools stay relevant and gain expanded usefulness. After that, suit upgrades, as your current chapter objective dictates.
