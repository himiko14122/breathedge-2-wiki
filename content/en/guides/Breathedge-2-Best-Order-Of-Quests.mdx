The **best order of quests in Breathedge 2** saves you from the most common early access mistake: burning oxygen backtracking across the first chapter. According to the [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2412960/Breathedge_2/), the first major story chapter already offers a large explorable area with main quests, side quests, and optional locations, which means the sequence you choose directly controls how many times you cross the same debris field. This guide ranks every objective by time efficiency, resource payoff, and narrative momentum, so you can move through the galaxy without wasting repair kits on avoidable detours.

## Why Quest Order Matters More Than You Expect

Breathedge 2 is not a linear corridor shooter. The opening chapter drops you into a sprawling zone where the **Coffinbot threat**, the **Breathedge Corporation** storyline, and the **Immortal Chicken** side content all compete for your attention at once. If you chase every marker in the order it appears, you will spend most of your early hours flying through empty space with a half-upgraded suit.

The core problem is **travel cost asymmetry**. Returning to a distant station for one missed objective often costs more oxygen and suit durability than completing two adjacent objectives in a single trip. Community reports from the early access launch consistently show that players who batch objectives by proximity finish the first chapter in roughly half the time of players who follow the journal strictly top-to-bottom.

### What the Journal Does Not Tell You

The in-game quest tracker lists objectives by acquisition time, not by logical sequence. That means a late-game side quest you picked up from a random NPC conversation can sit at the top of your list, pulling you away from a main mission that unlocks a **fast travel node** or a **ship upgrade** that would make every subsequent objective easier.

A better approach is to treat the quest list as a menu of options, not a checklist. Before committing to any destination, ask three questions: Does this objective unlock something reusable? Is there a secondary objective within the same sector? Will completing this first reduce the cost of a later objective? If the answer to all three is no, defer it.

| Priority Signal | What It Looks Like | Action |
|-----------------|-------------------|--------|
| **Unlock potential** | Objective rewards a ship module, fast travel point, or crafting recipe | Do immediately |
| **Proximity cluster** | Two or more objectives share a destination sector | Batch into one trip |
| **Narrative gate** | Main story mission that opens a new zone | Do before optional content in current zone |
| **Resource sink** | Side quest that costs more materials than it returns | Defer until crafting surplus |

## The Optimal Main Story Route Through Chapter One

The main story in Breathedge 2 is your backbone. It unlocks zones, introduces crew members, and gates most of the game's systems. But even within the main path, there is a smart sequence and a wasteful one.

### Step-by-Step Main Mission Order

Start with the **Coffinbot sabotage mission** that the opening cutscene pushes you toward. This is not just the narrative hook, it is the tutorial for combat movement and oxygen management. Delaying it means you fight the learning curve later with less margin for error.

Next, prioritize any objective that rewards a **suit integrity upgrade** or a **thruster efficiency boost**. The early game is defined by how far you can travel without stopping, and these two upgrades compound with every subsequent trip. According to the early access build, the first chapter includes core survival mechanics that reward players who stabilize their suit loop before branching into optional exploration.

The middle of the chapter is where most players stall. You will have three or four active threads, and the journal will not tell you which one unlocks the next zone. The reliable rule is to follow the **Babe investigation line** until it intersects with the **Breathedge Corporation war narrative**, because that intersection is what opens the second half of the explorable map. Everything else in the mid-chapter can wait.

### Main Story Priority Table

| Main Mission Thread | Why It Matters | When To Do It |
|--------------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Coffinbot first contact** | Teaches combat and oxygen loops | Immediately, no detours |
| **Suit upgrade chain** | Reduces travel cost for everything else | As soon as available |
| **Crew recruitment trigger** | Unlocks ship management and passive resource flow | Before long exploration trips |
| **Babe investigation** | Narrative gate to second map zone | Mid-chapter, before optional cleanup |
| **Corporation war resolution** | Opens endgame of chapter one | After side quest clusters are cleared |

## Batching Side Quests for Maximum Efficiency

Side quests in Breathedge 2 are not filler, but they are also not all equal. Some reward **one-of-a-kind junk items** that exist purely for humor, while others grant crafting blueprints that change your material economy. The trick is identifying which category a side quest belongs to before you spend the travel cost to reach it.

### The Hidden Locations and Easter Egg Economy

The game's official description confirms that exploration reveals **abandoned stations, ruined settlements, shipwrecks, and places no reasonable person would enter**. These are not randomly generated filler, they are hand-placed set pieces with unique loot and environmental storytelling. The problem is that they are scattered across a map that punishes inefficient routing.

This is where the Breathedge 2 hidden locations guide logic applies: treat every hidden location as a side quest with an invisible reward flag. Before leaving a sector, sweep for environmental tells: blinking station lights, debris trails that form a path, or unusual ship silhouettes. These often mark a location that contains a **strange device** or a **useful junk item** that a later quest will request.

| Side Quest Type | Typical Reward | Batching Strategy |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **NPC fetch requests** | Crafting materials, small blueprint unlocks | Batch with main story in same sector |
| **Crew personal goals** | Morale boosts, passive resource generation | Do when ship is already docked |
| **Environmental puzzles** | Unique modules, lore logs | Sweep when passing through, do not detour |
| **Immortal Chicken interactions** | Comedic items, crew reactions | Always active, triggers during other quests |
| **Optional combat arenas** | High-tier scrap, rare components | Save until suit is upgraded |

## Exploration Priorities and the Secrets Layer

Exploration in Breathedge 2 is not a side activity, it is the primary loop that feeds every other system. The official store page states that exploring the world is a major part of the experience, and the early access build confirms that optional locations, events, and assorted junk are intentionally dense. But dense does not mean you should clear everything immediately.

### What to Sweep, What to Skip, What to Save

The **secrets guide** approach divides the map into three layers. The **surface layer** is everything you can see from a normal flight path: stations, obvious wrecks, and quest markers. Clear this layer naturally as you move between objectives. The **signal layer** is hidden content that requires you to notice audio cues, light flickers, or movement in the background. Sweep this layer when you are already in a sector for a main mission. The **deep layer** is content gated behind upgrades or narrative triggers. Save this for the post-main-story cleanup phase.

Your first long exploration trip should only happen after you have the **suit integrity upgrade** and at least one **fast travel node** unlocked. Before that, long trips are mathematically wasteful. After that, a single well-routed trip can clear three or four hidden locations and two side quests in one oxygen cycle.

### Exploration Route Planning Checklist

- Identify the farthest objective you want to clear in this trip

- List every marker, hidden location, and side quest between your ship and that point

- Check which objectives require a resource you currently have

- Route from farthest point backward to your ship, not outward

- Leave 20% oxygen reserve for unexpected combat encounters

- Mark cleared sectors on the map so you never double back

Easter eggs in this game are not simple visual jokes, they are often multi-step environmental narratives that require you to be in specific places at specific times, or to carry specific items into specific locations.

### Trigger Chains and Conditional Events

Some easter eggs only activate after you have completed a seemingly unrelated quest. For example, a **strange device** found in an abandoned station might do nothing until you bring it to a specific crew member after a certain story beat. Community reports describe these as "soft-locked gags" that reward players who revisit old areas with new context.

The best order for easter egg hunting is to treat it as a **second pass activity**. Complete the main story first, then revisit sectors with your full kit and a mental list of oddities you noticed but could not interact with. The [npc dialogue guide](/en/story/breathedge-2-npc-dialogue/) covers how crew conversations hint at these hidden triggers, which is why you should exhaust dialogue options before leaving each story hub.

## Efficient Cleanup and the Pre-Endgame Sweep

Before you trigger the chapter one finale, there is a natural cleanup window. The main story will signal this moment, usually with a point of no return or a clear "finish preparations" prompt. This is your signal to pause and sweep everything you have deferred.

### The Final Sweep Order

Start with the **crew personal goals**, because those reward passive bonuses that will help during the final mission sequence. Then clear any remaining **hidden locations** in sectors you have not visited since your early game. Finally, wrap up the **easter egg chains** that you have partially triggered. Do not start new easter egg chains this late unless you know exactly where every step is located.

This is also the right time to invest in any **ship customization** you have been delaying. A fully repaired ship with optimized storage makes the finale significantly smoother. The [vehicle controls guide](/en/spaceships/breathedge-2-vehicle-controls/) details how planetary vehicle handling changes when you carry more cargo, which matters if you are hauling cleanup loot into the final stretch.

| **Remaining hidden locations** | Unique loot and lore closure | Medium, requires travel |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I do every side quest before the main story?**

No. Side quests that unlock crafting blueprints or crew bonuses are worth doing early, but pure fetch quests with cosmetic rewards can wait. Follow the main story until you unlock a fast travel node and a suit upgrade, then batch side quests by sector proximity.

**How do I know if a hidden location is worth detouring to?**

Look for environmental tells like blinking lights, debris paths, or unusual silhouettes. These usually indicate a location with unique loot. If a location requires a long detour and has no visual signal, it is likely a mid-game or post-story discovery point, not an early game priority.

**What is the best order of quests for the Immortal Chicken content?**

The Immortal Chicken content is passive and triggers during other quests, so you do not need to plan around it. Simply keep the chicken alive, interact with it when it appears, and check crew dialogue afterward. Its events weave into the main story naturally rather than forming a separate quest chain.

**Can I miss easter eggs permanently?**

Some easter eggs are tied to specific story beats and cannot be triggered after you pass certain points. The safe approach is to exhaust dialogue options with every NPC before advancing the main story, and to revisit sectors once after the mid-chapter story shift. Community reports suggest most gags remain accessible, but a few are missable.

**Is exploration required to finish chapter one?**

No, but skipping exploration leaves you under-equipped. You can finish chapter one with minimal side travel, yet resource scarcity will throttle your suit-upgrade loop before the finale. Optional stations, shipwrecks, and abandoned settlements feed crafting, so route them early in your quest order to avoid scraping by on quest rewards alone.
