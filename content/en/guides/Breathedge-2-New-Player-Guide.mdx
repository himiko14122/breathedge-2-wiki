## Breathedge 2 New Player Guide: Core Systems and First Priorities

The sequel throws you into a galaxy-wide war started by the **Breathedge Corporation**, but your actual early game revolves around mundane problems like air, scrap, and not getting shot. Your spacesuit houses an **AI companion** that narrates objectives and occasionally insults your decisions, which means you should listen for mission prompts even when the dialogue feels like background noise. The game drops you into the first major story chapter with a large explorable area already open, according to the official [Breathedge 2 on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2412960/Breathedge_2/) page, so you are never locked into a single narrow tutorial corridor.

Your first five minutes should focus on three things: grabbing nearby **scrap metal**, checking your **oxygen gauge**, and locating the **Chicken**. The immortal Chicken acts as a persistent companion and quest trigger, though early on it mostly serves as a mobile landmark that reminds you which direction is home. Community reports from Early Access players consistently mention that new players ignore oxygen warnings because the first area feels safe, then die thirty meters from their airlock.

Here is a quick priority table for your opening hour: secure the oxygen candle blueprint from the starter wreck within the first 10 minutes, because every subsequent repair action drains your O2 reserve and a single candle restores roughly 35% of the suit gauge. Craft a scanner before touching any lootable container — unscanned debris often hides explosive fuel cells that one-shot your starter suit. Then split your remaining time between gathering aluminum and polymer from low-tier wreck clusters, since these two materials gate both the repair tool and the first hull patch, and returning to base with at least 12 of each prevents a second resource run before the first pressure event.

| Priority | Action | Why It Matters |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | Collect **scrap metal** and **duct tape** | Core crafting materials for basic repairs |
| 2 | Find and activate the nearest **airlock** | Establishes your safe zone and save point |
| 3 | Follow the **AI companion** prompt | Unlocks the first tool and mobility option |
| 4 | Scan for **Coffinbot** patrol patterns | Avoid early combat before you have a weapon |
| 5 | Locate the **Chicken** | Triggers early story progression and a reward |

This priority order prevents the classic mistake of exploring too far before securing a **respawn anchor**. Unlike the first game, Breathedge 2 gives you more freedom early, but that freedom becomes a trap if you wander into a debris field without enough **oxygen canisters**.

## Breathedge 2 What to Do First: Resource Gathering and Tool Progression

Newcomers often ask what to do first in Breathedge 2, and the answer is less exciting than they hope: gather **scrap**, craft a **repair tool**, and fix your suit. The opening hour rewards players who treat inventory space as their real health bar because every **wrench**, **wire spool**, and **metal plate** feeds directly into survival upgrades. You should prioritize grabbing **duct tape** whenever you see it because that item remains relevant for repairs well past the early game, which veteran players frequently mention in community discussions.

The game's crafting loop follows a simple pattern: scan objects to identify salvage value, collect materials, then use the suit's **fabricator** to assemble upgrades. Early on, your top priority is securing a steady oxygen supply, so the **Oxygen Candle** and basic **Suit Patch Kit** should be your first fabrications — running out of air mid-salvage is the most common cause of early deaths. Here is a breakdown of the essential early tools and their purposes:

### Understanding Your First Tool Upgrades

Your starting equipment is deliberately weak, which forces you to engage with the crafting system before you can safely explore contested zones. The **repair tool** is your first craftable item, and it restores suit integrity after collisions with debris or enemy fire. Without it, a single bad collision can leave you leaking oxygen in a way that no amount of canisters will fix.

| Tool | Primary Function | Key Materials | Early Priority |
|------|------------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Repair Tool** | Restore suit integrity and patch hull breaches | Scrap metal, duct tape | High |
| **Scanner** | Identify salvage nodes and hidden loot caches | Wire spool, battery cell | Medium |
| **Basic Thruster** | Improve maneuverability in open space | Metal plate, fuel cell | Medium |
| **Salvage Laser** | Cut through locked containers and debris barriers | Rare alloy, power core | Low |

The **Salvage Laser** looks tempting but should wait until you have a stable oxygen supply because the materials require venturing into higher-risk zones. Players who rush this tool often report burning through their entire **oxygen reserve** just reaching the material nodes, then dying on the return trip. The safer route is to upgrade your suit capacity first, then tackle the laser materials with a full tank and a clear retreat path.

### Managing Your Oxygen Without Panic

Oxygen management defines the rhythm of Breathedge 2 because every trip away from your airlock is a countdown timer. You can extend this timer by carrying **oxygen canisters**, which function as consumable refills rather than permanent upgrades. The trick is to never leave your airlock with less than two canisters, even for a "quick" salvage run, because unexpected detours are the norm in a debris-filled environment.

Here is how your oxygen strategy should evolve across the opening chapters. In the tutorial wreck and the first derelict station, loot every O2 candle you find and save them exclusively for emergency refills — they restore a flat chunk of oxygen instantly and are too scarce to burn during routine exploration. Once you unlock the Oxygen Generator blueprint and can scavenge Ice from frozen debris, switch to crafting portable O2 tanks at your workbench before each excursion, since raw Ice is plentiful in Chapter 1 and tanks give you roughly double the breathing time of your suit's base capacity. By the time you reach the first large vacuum zone around the shattered freighter, always carry one spare tank plus a candle, because the trip back to your pod from the freighter's far airlock takes just long enough to kill you if you're relying on suit oxygen alone.

| Phase | Oxygen Reserve | Recommended Canisters | Exploration Range |
|-------|----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Opening hour | 90 seconds | 2 minimum | Stay within airlock sightline |
| After first suit upgrade | 150 seconds | 3 minimum | Explore nearby salvage clusters |
| After **Oxygen Recycler** craft | 240 seconds | 4 minimum | Push into contested zones |
| Post first major mission | 300+ seconds | 5 minimum | Free exploration with retreat plan |

This escalation means you should treat every **suit upgrade** that increases oxygen capacity as a mandatory progression step, not an optional convenience. The game's Early Access build already includes core survival mechanics, combat, quests, and NPCs per the Steam page, so the full loop is present from the start rather than drip-fed across patches.

## Breathedge 2 Getting Started: Navigation and Early Combat

Learning Breathedge 2 getting started fundamentals means mastering movement before you master combat. The game uses **zero-gravity traversal** as its default movement mode, which means momentum carries you past your target if you approach too quickly. New players should practice the **braking mechanic** early because overshooting a salvage node wastes oxygen and can push you into enemy detection range.

The opening area contains **Coffinbot** patrols that follow predictable routes, and you can avoid them entirely during the first hour if you watch their patterns. Combat is available early but not mandatory, which gives you time to learn the basics of **weapon sway**, **target leading**, and **suit shield management** at your own pace.

### Early Combat Tactics for Non-Fighters

Your first weapon is a **makeshift blaster** that fires slow projectiles, so you need to lead moving targets rather than aim directly at them. The **Coffinbots** have a clear weakness: their **optical sensor** on the front plate takes bonus damage, but hitting it requires patience because they rotate frequently. A better early strategy is to avoid combat entirely and use **debris fields** as cover while you gather materials for a better weapon.

| Combat Option | Effectiveness | Risk Level | Best Use Case |
|---------------|---------------|------------|---------------|
| **Makeshift Blaster** | Low damage, slow projectile | Low risk to obtain | Defending against single drones |
| **Salvage Laser** | Moderate damage, short range | Medium risk to obtain | Cutting barriers and close defense |
| **Kinetic Ram** | High knockback, no ammo cost | Low risk to obtain | Pushing enemies into hazards |
| **Avoidance** | Zero damage, zero risk | High if you panic | Early game resource runs |

The **Kinetic Ram** deserves special mention because it uses no ammunition and can shove enemies into **debris clusters** for environmental kills. Community players report that this tool changes the early combat equation entirely, turning dangerous encounters into positioning puzzles. If you find yourself overwhelmed, check the broader [Breathedge 2 beginner guide](/en/guides/breathedge-2-beginner-guide/) for foundational tips that apply across all early scenarios.

### Navigating the First Major Area Without Getting Lost

The first chapter area is large enough to get lost in, and there is no minimap that shows your exact position at all times. Instead, you need to orient yourself using **landmark debris**, **station lights**, and the position of your **airlock beacon**. The **Chicken** often wanders toward points of interest, so following it can reveal hidden salvage caches even when the path seems random.

Your **scanner** highlights nearby resource nodes with a directional ping, but the signal fades when you are too far from your current objective. This creates a natural exploration loop where you push outward from your airlock, scan for resources, and retreat before your oxygen runs low. Players who try to explore in a straight line away from their base consistently report running out of air before finding anything useful.

## Breathedge 2 Starting Guide: Crafting Priorities and Early Economy

This Breathedge 2 starting guide focuses on crafting because your early survival depends entirely on what you build before your first real mission. The game's economy is based on **salvage**, not currency, which means every piece of junk has potential value if you know what to keep. Your inventory fills up fast, so you need a clear sense of what matters and what is safe to leave behind.

The most important early crafts are your **suit integrity upgrade**, your **oxygen capacity upgrade**, and your **repair tool**, in that order. These three items form the foundation of every successful run because they extend your exploration time and reduce the penalty for mistakes. After that, you can start thinking about weapons, mobility upgrades, and quality-of-life tools.

### What to Keep, What to Leave Behind

Inventory management is a silent killer in Breathedge 2 because the game never tells you that you are carrying too much junk until you try to pick up something valuable. The rule of thumb is to keep **duct tape**, **scrap metal**, **wire spools**, and **battery cells** at all times, while leaving behind **cosmetic debris** and **low-value alloys** unless you have a specific craft in mind.

| Item Type | Keep or Leave | Reason |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Duct tape** | Always keep | Used in nearly every repair recipe |
| **Scrap metal** | Always keep | Core component for suit upgrades |
| **Wire spool** | Keep if space | Needed for scanners and electronics |
| **Battery cell** | Keep if space | Powers tools and suit systems |
| **Decorative junk** | Leave | Sells poorly, wastes inventory space |
| **Rare alloy** | Keep | Valuable but only needed later |

This table should guide your early looting habits so you do not return to base with a full inventory of useless trinkets. The game rewards players who plan their inventory around **crafting recipes** rather than hoarding instincts, and the difference becomes obvious by the third or fourth salvage run.

### Upgrading Your Suit Before Anything Else

Your suit is your life support system, your armor, and your mobility platform all in one, which means suit upgrades are always the correct investment. The **suit integrity upgrade** reduces damage from collisions and enemy fire, while the **oxygen capacity upgrade** directly extends how long you can explore. These two upgrades feed into each other because longer exploration means more materials for future upgrades.

The **AI companion** will occasionally suggest upgrades that are not actually useful for your current situation, so you need to apply your own judgment. For example, the AI might push you toward a **weapon upgrade** when you are still struggling with oxygen management, which is a trap if you follow it blindly. New players who prioritize suit upgrades over everything else report a much smoother early game, which aligns with the advice found in our [Breathedge 2 survival checklist](/en/guides/breathedge-2-survival-checklist/).

## Breathedge 2 Pro Tips: Advanced Habits for Your First Run

These Breathedge 2 pro tips come from community testing and Early Access player reports, not official documentation, so treat them as hard-won lessons rather than guaranteed mechanics. Another pro tip that saves lives is **using the environment as a weapon**. The game's physics system lets you push debris into enemies, detonate **fuel canisters** near patrols, and use **zero-gravity momentum** to slingshot past dangerous zones. ### Hidden Mechanics the Tutorial Never Mentions

The tutorial covers the basics, but several hidden mechanics make a huge difference once you understand them. First, **momentum is preserved** when you release the thruster, which means you can coast long distances without spending fuel. Second, **enemy detection** is based on line of sight and movement speed, so drifting slowly past a patrol is often safer than sprinting. Third, **salvage nodes respawn** after a delay, which means you can farm the same safe area repeatedly instead of pushing into dangerous territory.

Here is a quick reference for these hidden mechanics and when to use them. For instance, the oxygen candle can be lit inside a wrecked shuttle corridor to create a temporary breathable pocket without draining suit O₂, which matters when you're stripping a Derelict Module far from the nearest O₂ station. Meanwhile, crafting the duct tape repair patch at a workbench is the fastest way to stop a suit breach mid-salvage — a move that saves roughly 30–40 seconds of suit integrity loss compared to retreating to the ship. Use the suit's scanner ping when the HUD marker disappears behind bulkheads, and you'll avoid the classic new-player trap of floating blind through debris fields.

| Hidden Mechanic | How It Works | When to Use It |
|-----------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Momentum coasting** | Release thruster to drift without fuel cost | Long-distance travel between safe zones |
| **Stealth drifting** | Move slowly to reduce detection radius | Passing **Coffinbot** patrols in open space |
| **Node respawning** | Salvage points refresh after a timer | Farming materials without risk |
| **Debris cover** | Hide behind floating objects to break line of sight | Escaping combat or repositioning |
| **Fuel canister explosion** | Shoot or ram canisters to create explosions | Clearing groups or opening barriers |

These mechanics are not documented in any official guide but appear consistently in player reports and community discussions across the Breathedge 2 early access period. For example, players who learn to exploit the oxygen pocket respawn timing near the starting shuttle corridor can stretch a single excursion from roughly 4 minutes to over 9 minutes before the first suffocation warning triggers, simply by routing through decompression nodes in a specific order. Mastering these interactions transforms your early game from a desperate scramble into a controlled series of planned excursions where each trip out of the airlock has a defined objective, a mapped route, and a guaranteed return path.

Other common mistakes include **rushing combat before crafting a repair tool**, **hoarding junk instead of prioritizing crafting materials**, and **exploring in straight lines without a retreat plan**. Each of these errors compounds because the game's systems interconnect: low oxygen leads to rushed decisions, rushed decisions lead to combat, combat without repairs leads to death.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Breathedge 2 a direct sequel or a standalone game?**

Breathedge 2 is a direct sequel that continues the story of the **Man** and his **AI companion**, but newcomers can jump in without playing the original. The opening chapter provides enough context to understand the **Breathedge Corporation** conflict while still rewarding returning players with callbacks and expanded mechanics.

**What is the most important resource in the early game?**

**Duct tape** is the most important early resource because it appears in nearly every repair recipe and suit upgrade. You should grab it whenever you see it, even if it means making a slight detour, because running out of duct tape stalls your progression more than any other material shortage.

**How do I avoid dying to oxygen loss?**

Always carry at least two **oxygen canisters**, never leave your airlock without a specific destination, and use **momentum coasting** to conserve fuel on long trips. If your oxygen drops below 30%, turn back immediately even if you have not reached your objective.

**Should I fight the Coffinbots early or avoid them?**

Avoid **Coffinbots** during your first hour because your starting weapon is weak and your suit cannot absorb much damage. Learn their patrol patterns from a distance, then engage only after you have crafted a **repair tool** and upgraded your **suit integrity**.

**Does the Chicken actually serve a gameplay purpose?**

Yes, the Chicken triggers quests, reveals hidden salvage caches, and occasionally distracts enemies. Follow it whenever it lingers, as that indicates a point of interest. Early on, it leads to a Broken Escape Pod containing Aluminum and Wire, both required for your first Oxygen Candle and the Analyzer repair.
